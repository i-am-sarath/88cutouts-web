A corner cluster is useful when the photograph should remain the main attraction. Instead of scattering small objects across the entire image, group them in one region and leave most of the frame untouched.

## Choose the quietest corner

Look at the photograph before choosing the assets. Find a corner with little important detail and enough contrast for your cutouts. Avoid a face, a hand holding something meaningful, or a sign that explains the location.

![A larger accent with two smaller companions near one corner, leaving the central subject region open.](/guides/corner-cluster.svg)

If all four corners contain important information, put the photograph on a larger background canvas and place the cluster in the added margin instead.

## Establish the anchor

Use the largest object as the anchor and overlap it with one smaller object. Place a third tiny accent nearby, leaving a visible gap. The group should have a clear silhouette rather than looking like several unrelated items that happen to touch.

Try a visible width of about one fifth of the canvas for the main object, then adjust by eye. This is a design exercise, not a size requirement. Long, narrow assets may need different dimensions to carry similar visual weight.

## Avoid tangencies

A tangency occurs when two outlines almost touch without clearly overlapping or separating. At small sizes, the gap can disappear and create an awkward shape. Move the objects decisively apart or overlap them enough to establish a clear front and back.

Apply the same check to the canvas edge. An object barely clipping the boundary can look accidental. Either keep it fully inside or crop it confidently, while preserving its recognisable part.

## Balance with a lighter echo

If the corner makes the whole image feel tilted, add a much smaller accent elsewhere or move the caption slightly. Do not automatically build an equally large cluster in the opposite corner; that often produces two competing focal points.

View the finished image at phone size and check whether you still notice the photograph first. Remove one object if the cluster is reading as the subject.
