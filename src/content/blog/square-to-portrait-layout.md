Changing a canvas from square to portrait is a composition problem, not just a resize command. Stretching the finished square fills the new frame but distorts photographs and lettering. Instead, move the original elements into a new arrangement.

## Choose what should stay the same

Keep the main photo's proportions and the relationship between the heading and its supporting text. Those are usually more important than preserving every sticker's exact position.

![A square layout rearranged into a portrait frame with additional room for a caption rather than stretched elements.](/guides/reframe.svg)

For a worked exercise, adapt a 1080 × 1080 design to 1080 × 1920. The width can remain the same while the added height becomes useful space above or below the photograph.

## Give the extra area a role

Use the new region for a short heading, a personal note or breathing room around the subject. Avoid filling it with duplicates of existing stickers simply because it looks empty in the editor.

If the square contains a tightly cropped photograph, place it as a square panel within the portrait canvas. A neutral background can make that choice look intentional without inventing image detail beyond the original crop.

## Reposition related objects together

Move a caption and its attached bow as a group, then adjust their spacing. Moving every element independently can break the small relationships that made the original design work.

After repositioning, check edge crops and tangencies. An accent that was safely inside the square may now sit awkwardly against a new photo border.

## Recheck the hierarchy

The extra space may make the original heading feel too small, even though its pixel size has not changed. Compare the overall balance at normal viewing size before enlarging it. Sometimes moving the heading closer to the photo restores the connection without changing its size.

Keep the important content clear of areas the destination app may cover, and inspect the final preview there. A layout diagram cannot guarantee a universal safe area across interfaces.

Save the portrait version separately so the square remains usable. The [layer-order guide](/blog/layer-order-collage/) helps keep the source editable, and the [negative-space exercise](/blog/negative-space-collage/) offers alternatives to filling every added region.
