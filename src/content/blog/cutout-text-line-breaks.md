A decorative phrase often becomes difficult to read because it has too many letters on one line. Reducing every letter to fit solves the width problem while creating a readability problem. A deliberate line break is usually the better first experiment.

## Break at a meaningful boundary

For `HAPPY BIRTHDAY MAYA`, compare `HAPPY BIRTHDAY / MAYA` with `HAPPY / BIRTHDAY MAYA`. The first makes the person's name a separate emphasis; the second makes the greeting more evenly distributed. Choose the reading order you want rather than relying only on equal character counts.

![A short decorative heading arranged on two lines above a smaller supporting line.](/guides/type-hierarchy.svg)

Avoid separating parts that belong together, such as a first name and surname or a date from its month. If a line break creates ambiguity, rewrite the phrase.

## Compare visible widths

Ten narrow letters can occupy less space than seven wide paper scraps. Judge the rendered phrase rather than its character count. Transparent margins around individual letter files also affect the first layout.

Start with two lines at the same visible letter height. If one line is much longer, try a different break before shrinking it dramatically. A small size difference can establish hierarchy; an extreme difference can make one line seem unrelated.

## Leave a clear line gap

Descenders, tall scraps and rotated corners can collide across lines. Check the closest points, not only the baseline spacing. The white space between lines should remain visible at the final display size.

If the paper itself overlaps intentionally, make sure the printed letterforms do not. A decorative scrap can sit behind another scrap without making the text difficult to read.

## Keep supporting information plain

Use cutout letters for the short emotional message. Put a time, address or long explanation in a simpler type treatment beneath it. This allows the heading to stay expressive while the practical information remains easy to scan.

Make two exports and compare them at phone size. Ask someone to read the wording without telling them what it says; if that is not possible, leave the design for a few minutes and return with fresh eyes.
