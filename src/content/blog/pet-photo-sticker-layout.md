The expression is usually the reason a pet photograph works. Decorations should give it context without obscuring the eyes, ears or posture. Start by marking the parts that explain the moment before choosing a sticker.

## Follow the direction of the gaze

If the animal looks to the right, try leaving open space on that side and placing a short caption there. If it faces the camera, use a lower corner or a margin outside the photograph.

![A protected central subject region with a caption and accents placed outside its silhouette.](/guides/corner-cluster.svg)

Treat the diagram as a placement map, not a rule about every photograph. A curled sleeping cat and a jumping dog have very different shapes and need different protected areas.

## Preserve the outline

Whiskers, ear tips and paws can extend beyond the obvious body shape. Avoid placing a sticker just against those edges, where the two outlines may merge. Either separate them clearly or create a deliberate overlap away from an important feature.

Use one small motif first. A heart near a caption or a paper label along the bottom may be enough. Adding several animal stickers to a photograph of an animal can create a second competing subject.

## Choose colour from the photograph

Pick a background or accent that separates from the fur. A beige decoration may vanish against a pale coat; a bright red object may attract more attention than the face. Test the local area where the sticker will sit.

If the photo background is cluttered, put the image on a plain canvas with a border and decorate that border. This preserves the photograph without requiring you to edit around every object behind the pet.

## Keep the joke short

A two- or three-word caption gives the expression room to work. Place longer context in the accompanying message. If you add a speech bubble, point it toward the subject without crossing the face or introducing an awkward line through the image.

Preview at a small size and hide the decoration for a moment. If the original expression becomes much clearer, reduce or move the accents.

The [light/dark contrast guide](/blog/light-dark-sticker-contrast/) and [negative-space exercise](/blog/negative-space-collage/) offer useful checks. Save a separate original so the decorated version never replaces the only copy of the photo.
