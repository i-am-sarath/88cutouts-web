A sticker can look clear on the library's preview and disappear on your photo. The relevant contrast is the contrast immediately around its outline, not the average brightness of the whole photograph.

## Test the actual landing area

Place a pale sticker over a dark part of the photo, then over a light part. Keep its size and opacity unchanged. Next try a darker sticker with a similar silhouette in both positions. This isolates contrast from other design choices.

![A simple cutout tested on two backgrounds with different lightness.](/guides/transparency-test.svg)

Inspect small features separately. The main body of a bow may remain visible while the ribbon tails vanish. A flower's centre may be distinct while its pale petals blend into a shirt.

## Move before modifying

Moving the object to a quieter patch is often enough. Avoid adding an outline or shadow immediately; those effects change the asset's character and may make a soft photographic cutout look like a hard badge.

If the composition needs the sticker in one specific location, try a restrained backing shape. A small paper scrap or plain panel can separate the object from a busy photo. Make the backing clearly intentional rather than almost identical to the sticker's contour.

## Watch internal and external contrast

External contrast separates the sticker from its surroundings. Internal contrast makes its details readable. Increasing the object's size may help internal detail without improving its separation from the background.

Conversely, a dark backing can restore the outline while the tiny details remain too small to read. Decide which feature matters. A decorative shell may only need its silhouette; a date label needs its letters.

## Make a realistic preview

Review the design at the size people will actually see. Do not rely on an enlarged desktop view. If the image will appear in a small message preview, test that approximate size too.

For meaningful text, provide the information in the accompanying message as well when possible. A decorative image should not be the only place someone can find an important time or address.
