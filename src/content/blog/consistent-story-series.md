A series feels connected when it repeats a few recognisable decisions. It becomes monotonous when every page uses the same crop, sticker positions and text length regardless of the content. Build a small system that leaves room for variation.

## Fix three things

Choose a palette, a caption style and a margin convention. For example: cream and blue with one coral accent, a plain two-line caption, and a generous outer margin. Keep these decisions stable across three practice pages.

![Three pages with different image arrangements connected by the same colour accent and caption treatment.](/guides/sequence.svg)

Then allow the photo size, decorative position and amount of empty space to change. A close-up may need no decoration; a plain scene may benefit from a small accent.

## Give each page a purpose

Write one sentence describing what each page contributes. If two pages do the same job, choose the stronger one or change the second page's focus. A sequence should develop, not merely accumulate.

For a personal event, the pages might introduce the moment, show a detail and end with a short message. For a creative process, they might show source material, assembly and final output.

## Repeat a motif sparingly

Use one small symbol or paper label as a visual thread. It can move within the margin system while retaining the same colour and scale. Avoid making the motif so large that it becomes more memorable than the content.

If the motif collides with a subject on one page, omit it there. Consistency is a useful convention, not a requirement to damage an individual image.

## Review the sequence together

Place small previews side by side. Check whether one page is much darker, busier or more text-heavy than the rest. That may be intentional, but it should have a reason, such as providing a clear ending.

Read all captions in order. Remove repeated information and check that the final page does not introduce something that requires another explanation.

Save numbered exports and an editable master for future additions. The [travel diary recipe](/blog/travel-photo-diary-layout/) gives a specific three-photo example. The [reusable template guide](/blog/reusable-personal-template/) explains how to preserve the system while replacing its content.
