Negative space is the area that lets the important part of a design stand apart. It does not have to be pure white or completely blank. A softly coloured wall, clear sky or untextured paper can all provide a quiet region.

## Reserve space before decorating

For a portrait practice canvas, reserve the middle third for a short message. Put temporary guides around it and treat them as a boundary: decorations can approach that region but should not intrude into the lettering.

![An open central panel with small decorations outside the reading area.](/guides/negative-space.svg)

Start with your busiest asset outside the reserved area. This makes it easier to evaluate the asset's impact than filling the frame first and trying to carve out space afterward.

## Separate empty area from useful space

A tiny empty gap between two dense clusters is technically unoccupied, but it may not help the composition. Useful space creates separation around the thing you want people to notice.

Try moving one cluster farther away from the caption. Compare that with shrinking both clusters while leaving them close. The first change increases separation; the second reduces visual weight. They solve different problems.

## Use an uneven distribution

You do not need equal decoration in every corner. Try a group of two objects at the bottom and a single small object near the top. Leave one side mostly open. The uneven distribution gives the eye somewhere to enter and somewhere to rest.

If the background photograph already has branches, signs or patterned clothing, count those as visual objects. Adding only two stickers can still create a crowded frame when the photo itself is busy.

## Compare at a small size

Reduce the preview until the entire design fits comfortably on a phone screen. Ask whether the caption reads as a single block and whether the subject's outline remains clear. If not, remove a decorative element or move it across the boundary you established.

Keep a copy of the fuller version so you can compare honestly. Empty space can feel uncomfortable while you are editing at a large zoom, yet become the strongest part of the small finished image.

Use this approach with [three-sticker layouts](/blog/three-sticker-layout/) or a [caption panel](/blog/readable-caption-panel/). The goal is to make the design's purpose visible, not to meet a fixed percentage of empty pixels.
