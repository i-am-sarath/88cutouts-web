A birthday design should make two things obvious: who it is for and the feeling of the greeting. Start with one photograph and a short message rather than trying to include every favourite sticker at once.

## Set up the practice canvas

Use a 1080 × 1920 portrait canvas for this exercise. Place the photograph in the middle region and reserve a generous strip above or below it for the greeting. These are composition dimensions, not a guarantee about an app's interface overlays.

![A portrait birthday layout with a photo region, two-line greeting and small corner accents.](/guides/birthday-layout.svg)

Choose a photo with space around the face. If a tight crop leaves no room for decoration, add a neutral background outside the photograph rather than covering the person.

## Give the name a separate line

Create a short `HAPPY BIRTHDAY` heading and put the name on its own line. The name can be larger or a contrasting colour. Avoid using elaborate cutout lettering for a long paragraph; the greeting should be understood at a glance.

Make the heading with the cut-out letters in the [scrapbook maker](/scrapbook/?tool=text) and add the photograph on the same page, or start from the ready-made [keepsake journal template](/scrapbook/keepsake-journal/). Keep the text as a separate layer until spelling and spacing are final.

## Decorate one region

Place a bow or flower near a corner of the text area, then add a much smaller accent on the opposite side. Keep both outside the face and away from the ends of the name.

If you add an age or date, make it intentional. Check that it is correct and that the recipient is comfortable having it shared. A public greeting does not need personal details to feel personal.

## Review the recipient's version

Preview the design at phone size. Check the name, face crop and final line of the greeting. If the message is long, put the personal note in the accompanying text instead of shrinking the whole design.

Save the finished image and an editable copy. Send a private preview if the photograph or wording could be sensitive. The design is still a personal project even when it looks polished; check the [licence](/license/) before adapting assets for a business promotion.

For variations, use the [two-colour palette](/blog/two-color-sticker-palette/) or the [corner-cluster layout](/blog/corner-cluster-layout/) while keeping the same basic photo-and-greeting structure.
