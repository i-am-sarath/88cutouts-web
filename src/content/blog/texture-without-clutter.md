Paper grain, printed patterns and worn edges can make a collage feel tactile. They can also compete with photographs and lettering. The useful question is not how much texture you can add, but where it helps explain the material.

## Give texture a surface

Choose one main textured region: the background paper, a small label or the letter scraps. Keep the other large regions calmer. This makes the texture belong to an object rather than appear as a filter over everything.

![A textured outer surface surrounding a quiet caption panel.](/guides/caption-panel.svg)

For a first exercise, use textured paper behind a plain photograph and a solid caption panel. In a second version, use a plain background and textured letter scraps. Compare which version keeps the message clearer.

## Match the scale of the texture

Fine grain may disappear when reduced, while a bold gingham pattern can dominate a small design. Inspect the texture at the size it will actually appear, not only at the full resolution of the source file.

If a repeated pattern looks distracting behind text, changing its opacity may help, but moving it out of the reading area is more reliable. The eye still follows high-contrast edges even when they are faint.

## Limit conflicting materials

Glossy satin, rough kraft paper, glitter and newspaper can all work separately. Using all four in equal amounts makes it difficult to understand the intended mood. Choose a dominant material and let another appear as a small contrast.

A smooth photograph often provides enough contrast to a paper background on its own. You may not need an additional patterned sticker simply to create variety.

## Compare with a plain version

Duplicate the composition and temporarily replace the textured surface with a solid colour of similar lightness. If the plain version reads much better, reintroduce only the part of the texture that adds something useful.

Check the saved export as well. Resampling and compression can make a previously subtle pattern look rough or uneven. Keep the original textured source in case you need a different scale later.
