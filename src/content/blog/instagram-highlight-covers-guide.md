Your Highlight covers sit directly under your bio, above the grid, permanently. They are the second thing a visitor's eye lands on after your profile picture, and unlike the grid they do not change on their own. That makes them the highest-leverage design surface on an Instagram profile — and the one most often left as a random frame from whatever Story happened to be first.

## The dimensions, and why they are confusing

The commonly quoted number is 1080 × 1920 px. The commonly quoted number is also right, and it confuses people because Highlight covers are displayed as small circles, not as tall rectangles.

Here is what actually happens:

1. You supply an image. If you use the "Edit Cover" flow, you can supply any image; if you pick a frame from a Story, it is already 1080 × 1920.
2. Instagram takes a **square from the centre** of that image, then displays that square as a **circle**.
3. That circle is rendered at roughly **112 px** on a profile page, and smaller in some contexts.

So the working area is a centred circle, and everything outside it is discarded. On a 1080 × 1920 canvas that circle is roughly **1080 px in diameter, centred vertically** — meaning the top and bottom ~420 px of your image are gone entirely.

**Why work at 1080 × 1920 anyway?** Because it lets you post the image as a Story and then select that Story frame as the cover, which is the flow with the fewest steps. If you use the Edit Cover route, a 1080 × 1080 square works just as well and wastes nothing.

## The mistake everyone makes

**Your icon needs to be much smaller than you think.**

The intuition is to fill the frame — you made a nice icon, you want it visible. But the circle is cropped from the centre, and then displayed at 112 pixels. An icon filling the frame gets its edges cut by the circle, and what remains is a claustrophobic close-up.

The workable proportions:

- **Icon width: about 30–35% of the canvas width.** On 1080px, that is roughly 320–380 px.
- **Centred both horizontally and vertically.**
- **Everything else: background.**

This feels wrong while you are making it and correct the moment you see it on a profile. Highlight covers are read as *symbols* at a glance, not as pictures, and symbols need surrounding space to read.

## Making a matched set

The whole value of Highlight covers is that they are a *set*. Consistency across five circles does more for a profile than any individual cover.

### 1. One background treatment for all of them

Pick one and apply it without variation:

- **Solid colour** — the simplest and hardest to get wrong.
- **A single gradient** — same two colours, same direction, on every cover.
- **A texture** — paper, linen, a subtle grain. Same texture, same scale.

Do not vary the background per Highlight unless the variation itself is the system — for example five tints of the same hue, in a deliberate order.

### 2. One icon treatment for all of them

- Same rendering style. All paper cutouts, or all pixel art, or all line icons. Never a mix.
- Same size, within about 10%.
- Same visual weight. An icon with lots of dark area next to one that is mostly outline will look wrong even if they are the same size.

### 3. Enough contrast to survive 112 pixels

The same [small-size logic that governs profile pictures](/blog/how-to-choose-a-profile-picture/) applies here, more severely — a Highlight cover is smaller than most profile pictures.

Convert your set to greyscale. If icon and background are similar values, they will merge into flat circles on a real profile. Pale icons on pale backgrounds are the most common failure, and they look particularly bad in dark mode where the whole row reads as a line of light blobs.

### 4. Choose icons that are legible as silhouettes

At 112 px you have a symbol, not an illustration. Things that work: a heart, a star, a sun, a bow, a camera, an envelope, a coffee cup, a single letter. Things that do not: a detailed scene, a photograph, anything with text beyond one word.

The [sticker library](/) here works well for this because the cutouts are already silhouette-driven — a [sun](/stickers/golden-sun/), a [film camera](/stickers/retro-film-camera/), a [sealed letter](/stickers/sealed-love-letter/), a [bow](/stickers/pink-satin-bow/) each read instantly at small size.

## A step-by-step method

**Using a phone only, no design app:**

1. Make a plain background at 1080 × 1080 — a solid colour block is fine — and save it to your camera roll.
2. Open any free editor: Canva, Picsart, or your phone's built-in markup tools.
3. Place your cutout dead centre, sized to about a third of the canvas width.
4. Export as JPEG at 1080 × 1080 and save it.
5. On your profile, long-press the Highlight → **Edit Highlight** → **Edit Cover** → the gallery icon → pick your file.
6. Repeat for each Highlight, changing only the icon.

There is no need to post the cover as a Story first. The Edit Cover route takes any image from your camera roll, and it does not add anything to the Highlight's contents.

**Using a design tool:**

Make one artboard, 1080 × 1080, with the background and a centred placeholder. Duplicate it five times, swap the icon in each, export all. Ten minutes for a full set, and you have a template for whenever you add a Highlight later.

**The most important step, either way:** before you commit, look at your own profile. Not the export, the actual profile page. That is the only place the set exists as a set, and problems that were invisible in your editor — one icon reading heavier, two that are hard to tell apart, poor contrast in dark mode — are obvious immediately.

## Naming them

Highlight names are limited to about 15 characters and are displayed in a small font under each circle. Practical guidance:

- **One word wherever possible.** "Travel", "Recipes", "FAQ", "Shop".
- **Consistent register.** All nouns, or all verbs. Mixing "Travel" and "Shop now" reads as inconsistent.
- **No emoji in the name if you have an icon.** The icon is already doing that job, and the emoji fights it.
- **Leave one blank only deliberately.** Some accounts use unnamed Highlights so the circles read as a pure graphic row. It looks clean and it costs the viewer information; make it a choice rather than an accident.

## How many, and in what order

**Five to seven.** Most phones show about five without scrolling, so anything beyond that is a second-class citizen. Beyond seven, the row reads as storage rather than navigation.

**Order matters and you control it indirectly.** Instagram orders Highlights by most recently updated, left to right. Adding a Story to a Highlight moves it to the front. If you want a specific order, add a Story to each Highlight in reverse order of how you want them displayed, then remove the Stories if you do not want them there.

**Put the highest-value one first.** For most accounts that is either the one that explains what the account is, or the one that converts — an FAQ, a shop, a portfolio.

## Content that belongs in a Highlight

A separate question from design, but the covers are wasted if what is behind them is not worth opening.

Good candidates: an introduction to the account, frequently asked questions, testimonials or reviews, a portfolio or process, product categories, a location or venue guide, a recurring series.

Poor candidates: a chronological dump of everything, anything time-sensitive that will be stale in a month, and reposts of grid content people have already seen.

**Prune periodically.** A Highlight with forty Stories in it does not get watched to the end. Ten to fifteen is a comfortable ceiling, and pruning is faster than curating.

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover looks zoomed in | Icon fills too much of the frame | Reduce icon to ~1/3 of canvas width |
| Icon is off-centre | Working area assumed to be the whole canvas | Centre both axes; the crop is centred |
| Covers look unrelated | Mixed icon styles or backgrounds | One background, one icon style, one size |
| Row disappears in dark mode | Low value contrast | Darken backgrounds or lighten icons |
| Cannot change cover | Looking in the wrong menu | Long-press Highlight → Edit Highlight → Edit Cover |
| Cover added an unwanted Story | Used the Story-frame route | Use Edit Cover and pick from the camera roll instead |

## Tying it to the rest of the profile

The strongest profiles treat the [profile picture](/dp-maker/), the Highlight covers and the grid as one system — same palette, same rendering style, same level of contrast. That is not extra work if you decide the palette once; it is the same work applied consistently, which is the whole argument in [building a cohesive aesthetic](/blog/build-a-cohesive-instagram-aesthetic/).

Practically, the easiest version: pick five cutouts from a single [category](/), put them on one background colour, and use a sixth from the same category in your profile picture. The set will read as designed, and it takes about fifteen minutes.
