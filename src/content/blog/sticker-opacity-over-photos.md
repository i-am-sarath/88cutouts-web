An opacity percentage has no fixed visual meaning on its own. A pale bow at 70% may almost disappear on a white shirt, while a dark bow at the same setting remains obvious against the sky. Choose the setting over the intended photograph, not only over a checkerboard.

## Compare three starting points

Place a sticker over a quiet area of your image and compare 100%, 70% and 40%. Treat these as a useful experiment, not prescribed settings. At full opacity, the sticker behaves as a separate object. Lower values mix its colour with the underlying image.

![One accent shown at full, medium and low opacity over the same background.](/guides/opacity.svg)

On 88cutouts, the sticker page's opacity control changes the copied or downloaded result. Keep an original 100% version if you may want a stronger overlay later. Reducing opacity in a saved file throws away the original strength unless you keep another copy.

## Know what the slider cannot fix

If a sticker covers a face, lowering opacity leaves a ghost across the face. Moving or shrinking it is usually a cleaner fix. If its edges have a white halo, lowering opacity weakens the halo and subject together; it does not repair the edge colour.

Fine outlines can disappear before the main shape does. Inspect ribbon tails, small lettering and whiskers separately from the sticker's centre.

## Watch stacked transparency

Two translucent objects can produce a darker intersection. For two identical 50%-opaque layers placed exactly over each other, the combined opacity over an otherwise transparent background is 75%, not 100%: `1 − (1 − 0.5) × (1 − 0.5)`.

This matters when you duplicate a faint sticker to make a border. Overlapping ends can become visible seams. Either separate the objects slightly or use one continuous border design.

## Check on the destination background

Download the result and reopen it over both a light and dark layer. Then place it on the actual photo. A faint overlay designed for a dark photograph may not transfer well to a bright one.

If your goal is a readable caption, keep the lettering strong and reduce the surrounding decoration instead. For a worked arrangement, try the [quiet caption panel](/blog/readable-caption-panel/) or use a [sticker pack](/packs/) to compare several motifs before committing.
