A caption over a textured photograph can become hard to read even when the lettering is large. A plain panel gives the text a consistent background and creates an intentional place for decoration.

## Size the panel around the message

Start with the exact words, including names and dates. On a 1080-pixel-wide practice canvas, try a panel 720 pixels wide with 48 pixels of inner padding on each side. That leaves 624 pixels for text. If the message wraps awkwardly, shorten it or increase the panel height before squeezing the letters.

![A caption panel with an inner text region and a clearly marked padding boundary.](/guides/caption-panel.svg)

The measurements are a starting layout, not a platform safe-zone specification. Destination interfaces can cover different areas, so inspect the final design inside the app before publishing.

## Keep background variation outside the text

Begin with an opaque panel. If you want a translucent look, lower its opacity gradually while checking the busiest part of the photograph underneath. A panel that looks readable over sky may fail over hair or patterned clothing.

Use a calm text colour with strong contrast. Do not make every word a different colour simply because the stickers are colourful. One accent word is easier to balance than a rainbow of competing emphasis.

## Attach decoration to the edge

Place a bow, tape scrap or flower across an outer corner of the panel. Let it overlap the panel border, but keep it outside the text's padded area. This makes the decoration feel connected without turning it into an obstacle to reading.

If the sticker has long ribbon tails, rotate or resize it until the tails avoid punctuation and the ends of lines. Small collisions are more distracting than an obvious, deliberate overlap.

## Read the exported version

Save a copy and view it at normal phone size. Read the whole message aloud, including numbers. Check that line breaks do not separate a first name from its surname or an event time from its qualifier.

For an invitation, repeat essential details in the accompanying text message. The image can carry the visual mood while ordinary text remains easy to copy and search.
